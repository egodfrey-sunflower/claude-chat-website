2Encode a digit

The encoder doesn't map an image to a single point. It outputs a Gaussian q(z|x) with mean μ and standard deviation σ. To decode, we sample from it with the reparameterisation trick: z = μ + σ ⊙ ε, where ε ~ N(0, I).

■ encoder q(z|x)   ■ decoder p(x|z), with each pixel modelled as a Bernoulli probability

A VAE is a latent-variable model: imagine every digit was made by picking a hidden code z from a simple prior p(z) = N(0, I) and passing it through the decoder. We can't compute log p(x) directly, so we train the encoder and decoder together to maximise a lower bound on it, the evidence lower bound (ELBO):

log p(x) ≥ 𝔼q(z|x)[ log p(x|z) ] − KL( q(z|x) ‖ p(z) )

The first term rewards accurate reconstructions. The second keeps each image's code distribution close to the prior. That's why the codes pack into a disc around the origin, why the space has few holes, and why decoding a random point from N(0, I) gives something digit-like. The reparameterisation trick moves the randomness into ε, so gradients can flow through μ and σ and the whole thing trains with ordinary backpropagation. That was the key idea of Kingma & Welling's 2013 paper.

Things to notice

  • Smooth morphing. Drag slowly between clusters: a 7 becomes a 9 becomes a 4, with plausible in-between digits all the way.
  • Overlap. The unsupervised model never sees a label, and it spends its two numbers on whatever explains the most pixels. Slant and stroke width change lots of pixels, while a 4 and a 9 differ by a few near the top, so 4s and 9s (and 3s, 5s and 8s) end up sharing territory.
  • The edges. Far outside the 2σ ring the prior has almost no mass, so the decoder was barely trained there and its digits stretch and blur. Try the decoded grid.
  • Blurriness. A Bernoulli or Gaussian decoder averages over what it's unsure of. That's why VAE samples look soft, and partly why GANs and diffusion models took over image generation. Modern systems such as Stable Diffusion still use a VAE, but as a compressor whose latent space a diffusion model learns.
  • Draw something that isn't a digit. The encoder still has to put it somewhere, and the decoder answers with its nearest digit-shaped guess.

The α models are the same VAE trained jointly with a classifier. A single linear layer reads the sampled z and predicts the digit, and its cross-entropy is added to the loss:

loss = −ELBO + α · cross-entropy( label, classifier(z) )

Everything else is identical: the same architecture, the same random initialisation, the same order of training data and 60 epochs. The only difference is α. Because the classifier reads a noisy sample rather than μ, classes have to be separated by more than the encoder's σ. Because it's linear, its decision regions on the map are straight-edged wedges. ELBO, reconstruction and KL are average nats per test image (higher ELBO is better). Accuracies are on the 10,000 test digits. Tap a row to switch model.

  • α = 1 is a nudge. The map looks much like the unsupervised one. The classifier's regions slice through clusters rather than around them, so it only gets 67% right. The ELBO doesn't suffer; it's 0.6 nats better, which may be run-to-run noise.
  • α = 10 makes spokes. Classes start radiating out from the centre. 3s and 5s pull apart: the nearest-neighbour score goes from 74% to 92% for 3s and from 61% to 87% for 5s. 4, 7 and 9 are still tangled together. There's still no ELBO cost.
  • α = 100 makes petals. Ten clusters sit around the origin, one per digit. A linear classifier reading just two numbers gets 96.8% of test digits right, and every digit scores above 95%. Style still varies within each petal; try dragging through the 1s in the grid.
  • The price. To keep classes apart even after noise is added, the encoder pushes codes away from the prior. The map's range grows from ±3.5 to ±5, KL rises from 6.9 to 10.5 nats, and the ELBO drops by 4.3 nats. Reconstruction is almost unchanged. The middle of the map, where the prior puts most of its mass, becomes a crossroads of blended digits, so a z drawn from N(0, I) is more likely to land between classes.
